My time and energy may be split across several subjects, diluting myself from focusing on one; however, it has shaped me into being a more well-rounded individual with a versatile skillset and background with the ability to see the world through a sharper lens for why it is today, where it's heading, and position accordingly. That shift led me to Teladoc Health, the largest virtual-care company in the world.

During this time, I've grown significantly, supported by mentors and shaped by experience across client experience & operations, program management, clinical operations, patient experience, and performance operations. I've led C-suite-level initiatives spanning product, engineering, clinical, and operations, resulting in multi-million-dollar revenue and new commercial opportunities. The breadth of my work and the people I've learned from have been an immense foundation as I've begun my career, for which I am grateful.

## Psychedelic medicine

Driven by curiosity and my neuroscience background at Penn, I've been drawn to the science of psychedelic medicine since 2021. In a neuroscience class on hallucinogens, including psilocybin and LSD, I first realized these "drugs" could have immense potential for healing and for advancing science. I became fascinated by the contrast between what the clinical literature suggests and my prior assumptions, shaped by mainstream perception.

Throughout my time at Penn, I continued learning to better understand the roots of that disconnect (see ["Medicine in History (Health and Societies) Final Project - 2021"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sJ1BDSdRUuc)) and the broader science behind psychedelic medicine. A major inflection point was [hearing personal healing stories directly from clinical trial participants](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ccD2FlAWYdE), which became my "why" and motivation to stay involved in this industry.

Even with that personal motivation, I initially felt lost, without an outlet to share these interests. That's what led me to restart a psychedelic society during my graduate studies at Rutgers University (see "[RU Psyched Club Manual](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1NF2iuvrhoLAkkLj_odi6kYI9i_AG8Q6w/view?usp=drive_link)") and to expand the mission on a global scale through the [Intercollegiate Psychedelics Network](https://www.intercollegiatepsychedelics.net/).

Today, I'm most passionate about expanding my reach in this space as psychedelic medicine enters the mainstream. I care about the path from clinical validation to real access, and I look forward to opportunities to work with individuals and companies to deliver psychedelic therapy safely and efficiently at scale.

## Investing & markets: Bitcoin, Technology/AI, Energy

In my spare time, I write and share market research at @jmjcapital across individual companies, macroeconomics, Bitcoin, energy, technology, and psychedelic medicine. I enjoy understanding businesses from the inside out and forming a view before the consensus does.

JMJ Capital started in 2021 as a joke among a few friends (JMJ stands for Justin, Mason, Jamie). As a neuroscience major, I didn't have a traditional educational background in finance, markets, or economics, so I leaned on friends, online communities, podcasts, books, and LLMs to build my understanding.

Today, I share this passion with a broader group of friends and an online community on X. The industries I research are rooted in my core philosophy of investing in areas that everyone needs, no one can stop, and few understand.

- **Bitcoin:** Often dismissed as magic internet money, Bitcoin is a savings technology that the world has never had before, and it addresses many societal issues that stem from weak forms of money (e.g. cost of living crises, wealth disparities, government exploitation of money). We exchange physical energy and work for a wage, then save and invest to retain purchasing power for future needs (e.g., a first home, retirement). Throughout history, we have used many forms of money (e.g. jewelry, seashells, metals, and fiat), each with trade-offs: non-scarce/easily reproduced, non-divisible, non-portable, and centralized. Bitcoin is the opposite and, most critically, solves what gold couldn't: portability and decentralization, while being even more scarce. That gives individuals more self-sovereignty over hard-earned money and protection from inflation and monetary debasement driven by government exploitation and inefficient capital allocation. Examples include [Venezuela's Hyperinflation Crisis of the 2010s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XbKQ9_RFIJc), Nazi Germany borrowing from the central bank to fund WWII, Roman Empire coin clipping to fund expansion, the Bretton Woods Act of 1934 seizing gold from Americans to only debase the USD by 40% the year later, and today's cost-of-living pressures tied to monetary debasement. Bitcoin, while not perfect, is a borderless monetary network with properties no prior form of money has combined: divisible, portable, non-corruptible, and decentralized, keeping it out of centralized control and overreach. People don't understand bitcoin because they don't understand money and haven't taken the time to trace the downstream effects of the money society has inherited.
- **Energy:** We have been going through an energy transformation over the last few decades, with a recent acceleration driven by electrification of the grid (e.g. electric vehicles, heat pumps) and, most notably, rising demand from artificial intelligence. While headlines focus on bringing more nuclear, oil, natural gas, and even coal onto the grid, many people miss what is actually happening. Despite waning policy support and incentives, solar, wind, and battery energy storage continue to dominate new grid capacity additions because they are available today and getting cheaper every year. What's often missed is the role of technological learning curves, which show how quickly costs for solar, wind, and storage have declined, especially relative to other technologies, due to a steeper learning curve. Within energy, I pay particular attention to battery energy storage. It can be paired with solar/wind to address intermittency, and it can also be paired with natural gas and nuclear to get more out of the existing grid by serving as a load balancer. That lets nuclear and natural gas run closer to nameplate capacity instead of being dialed down to balance supply and demand. Energy is a large market with persistent problems, and newer technologies like battery energy storage are an effective solution. These nuances are often overlooked, and many people miss the opportunity to invest in this transformation.
- **AI/Technology:** Until recently, it wasn't conventional wisdom to view artificial intelligence as truly disruptive, with many calling for a bubble akin to the 2001 dot-com era. Often, the people calling for a bubble are not regular users of the technology. Becoming a user of Claude, specifically Opus 4.5 and Openclaw when it was released, made it clear to me that we are in the early innings of a technological revolution. Coupled with my knowledge of energy, and more specifically the physical constraints and long lead times required to bring power onto the grid, I saw an opportunity to invest in the infrastructure driving AI. That led me to companies like IREN, CIFR, and WULF in 2024 as a way to invest in the physical buildout that both training and serving inference for AI models depend on.

While investing is critical for building wealth and keeping up with inflation and monetary debasement, it's also a way to develop a more complete view of the world and how companies operate. Through investing, I've learned patience, discipline, risk management, and critical thinking, lessons I can carry into personal and professional life.
